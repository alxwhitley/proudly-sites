## Platform

web

## Users

**Primary (majority, best evidenced) — build the site for this person.** Founder-operated, reputation-and-referral-led service business owner. Roughly 45–60, not design-literate. Reads a website in a 90-second gut check and trusts familiar, credible signals over novel or slick ones. Already does great work; the website is the weak link, not the business. Wants something that looks the part, feels legitimate, is easy for their clients to navigate, matches the brand in their head, and comes from someone who will not disappear.

**Secondary / ceiling buyer [HYPOTHESIS — rests on one client, Jubala].** Same founder psychology, higher complexity. Has usually already hit a wall: hired talented designers who could not deliver a site that actually worked (multi-location, online ordering, mobile, real navigation) while still looking clean. This is the $10k+ buyer, reached through proof (the Jubala case study), never led with in headline messaging — leading here optimizes for the minority and loses the majority.

**Triggering moment for both:** a referral is coming and the current site would undercut it; the business has outgrown its site; or a previous attempt failed (a generic DIY/template, or a designer who made it pretty but broken/hard to navigate at scale).

## Product Purpose

Proudly Sites is a solo-operator web design/build/copy/SEO service for reputation-led service businesses whose current website undercuts the caliber of work their clients already trust them for. This marketing site's job is to pass the buyer's 90-second gut check and convert them into a discovery call / client. Success is measured there, not in traffic or vanity metrics.

## Positioning

One person, founder-to-founder, no handoffs — the person on the discovery call writes the copy, designs the pages, and answers the emails. Brand, copy, design, and a functional build from one competent person (not claimed as "design + development," since the whole field already claims that). Craft-led, not funnel-led: the site is a reputation asset, not a lead machine. Custom Framer builds with SEO and AI-search-aware foundations — fast to launch, easy for the owner to update.

The differentiator is proven, not claimed:
- The Jubala case study carries the ceiling proof — four locations, online ordering, mobile, real navigation, and still clean.
- The portfolio is the primary conversion tool — client work is shown, with the site presented over it.
- A real person is on the page — real photo, direct-work relationship, one point of contact.
- Recognizable trust signals — real Google reviews, real client names, transparent process.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: **Proudly Sites**.
- Voice: warm, calm, confident, plain — a founder talking to another founder who faces the same pressures. No hype, no guru voice, no fake urgency.
- Banned/constrained language: never say "StoryBrand" in client-facing copy; no em dashes in copy drafts; do not lead with saturated wallpaper claims ("custom," "results-driven," "full-service," "we listen," "beautiful websites that convert," "no BS," "trusted"); never disparage other designers or agencies — state capability positively; "fast" is supporting language only, never a headline; no funnel/lead-gen/ads framing; no industry targeting in the core/main message (industry specificity lives in case studies and per-vertical SEO only).
- AI stance: hold a defined, proactive point of view on AI in web design across blog/social — where it helps an owner, where it costs them, why judgment/strategy/accountability still matter. Name the failure mode honestly, without an "AI slop" sneer, and never lead a conversion surface (homepage, hero, CTA) with the AI comparison.

## Product Principles

1. Prove competence, don't claim it — every differentiator (solo-operator model, functional-at-scale builds, craft) is demonstrated through case studies and specific detail, never asserted as a headline the whole field already uses.
2. Lead with the referral-and-reputation buyer, not the ceiling buyer — the majority-fit persona drives primary messaging; scale/complexity proof supports but never leads.
3. Trust over slickness — this buyer reads a site in a 90-second gut check and trusts familiar, credible signals over novel or clever ones; default to legible and credible over impressive.
4. One accountable person, always visible — the single-operator model is a structural fact, not a marketing angle, and should read as such everywhere it appears.
5. Don't overstate the evidence base — the client base is still small; positioning and proof claims should stay proportionate to actual evidence on hand.
