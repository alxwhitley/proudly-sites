## Overall Impression

The system-level discipline is real and rare: one accent color spent only on buttons, one shadow spent once per surface, hairline borders everywhere, link-hover-as-motion instead of color — held across all 13 routes, including the three new surfaces from this session (the case-study "See Live Site" button, the homepage Vital Watch fix, and the `/work` honey-gold quote band all read as native extensions of the system, not bolt-ons). The single biggest opportunity is that the site's own chrome and forms currently contradict its central promise ("confirm what the referral was told, no surprises") in ways a skeptical visitor hits almost immediately: two dead nav links, a form that cannot succeed, and a portfolio — the page positioning.md names as the primary conversion tool — that is half placeholder. None of these are hard fixes; all of them are more damaging to this specific trust-led buyer than a purely cosmetic issue would be.

## What's Working

1. **The One Voice / Single Tool rules actually shipped, sitewide** — very few real projects hold a restraint rule this consistently across 13 routes; the eye lands on exactly one thing per closing band, every time.
2. **Church Studio is a genuinely persuasive product page** — real client evidence in the first viewport, an honestly-explained price band, a disqualifying line for bad-fit buyers, a purpose-built inline form. It's the fullest execution of the brand's core claim on the site.
3. **This session's three additions read as native, not bolted-on** — the "See Live Site" button matches the nav CTA exactly and the pending state is honest rather than a dead link; the honey-gold quote band is a genuine authored moment (both assessments independently flagged it as a highlight) that breaks the site's own rule in exactly one deliberate, documented place rather than eroding it.

This is the site's only conversion mechanism. **Fix:** insert the real Web3Forms access key (already tracked in `plan.md`'s morning queue). **Command:** `/impeccable harden`, then verify the success path end-to-end.

**[P1] Primary nav sells two dead links on every page.** "Pricing" (`/#pricing`) and "Blog" (`/#blog`) appear in `Header.astro`, the mobile menu, and `Footer.astro` — no such ids exist anywhere in `src/`. For a brand whose pitch is trust-verification, the header itself fails the referral test on every single page load. **Fix:** remove both until the sections/pages exist, or point Pricing at the one real price on the site (Church Studio's). **Command:** `/impeccable clarify`.

**[P1] Mobile nav accordion still doesn't collapse.** `global.css:125` — `.mobile-submenu { display: flex }` overrides the `hidden` attribute, so both submenus render fully expanded the instant the hamburger opens; `aria-expanded="false"` lies to assistive tech; the primary CTA is pushed below the 844px fold. Carried unchanged from the prior critique snapshot (2026-08-08T02-29-37Z). **Fix:** `.mobile-submenu:not([hidden]) { display: flex; }`. **Command:** `/impeccable adapt`.

**[P1] Legacy Renovations' proof image contradicts its own case copy.** The case narrative and summary describe a kitchen-and-bath renovation brand; `legacy-renovations.jpeg` (used on 6 surfaces: home, `/work`, `/services`, both `/services/branding` and `/services/web-design`, and the case hero) actually shows a "Restore Your Surfaces with Confidence — Expert Tile Repair" hero with a lead-capture form. This is the same family of finding as the 2026-08-07 `/services` critique's P0 (mismatched screenshot vs. caption), now confirmed present on the case page itself too. **Fix:** capture the actual kitchen/bath pages of the live site, or rewrite the surrounding copy to match what the asset actually shows. **Command:** `/impeccable polish` (blocked on the real capture).

**[P2] The portfolio — positioning's named "primary conversion tool" — is half placeholder.** 2 of 4 `/work` cards are beige "Live-site capture pending" panels; Strictly Clean's case hero opens with a full-width empty panel directly under its H1.
